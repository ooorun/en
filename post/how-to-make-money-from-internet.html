An Ordinary Person's Guide to Internet Gold Mining: Getting Started with Indie Development

2025-08-15 #Indie Development#Personal Projects#Side Hustle

Have you ever envied those indie developers who share their income transparently on social media and achieve financial freedom through personal projects? Seeing their monthly income easily reaching thousands of dollars, you might feel that it is an unattainable goal.

But the truth is, successful indie development is not an exclusive game for geniuses. Behind it lies a methodology that ordinary people can also learn and practice. The key to success is rarely a earth-shattering “genius idea,” but rather the right strategy and persistent action.

This article will lay out a practical, low-cost indie development roadmap, aiming to help every ordinary person with an idea start their own internet gold mining journey from zero to one.

Even if you don't understand technology, it doesn't matter. AI is developing rapidly, and various programming assistants are emerging constantly. In the AI era, everyone can be a product manager.

Standing on the Shoulders of Giants: Choosing a Mature Tech Stack

When choosing programming languages and frameworks, a common misconception is to chase “future trends,” trying new languages or frameworks that you are unfamiliar with but seem cutting-edge. This often results in low efficiency, running into many pitfalls, and having no one to ask for help.

The best strategy is: Choose currently popular programming languages, which means:

  • Large Developer Community: Taking JavaScript (Node.js), Python, Java, etc., as examples, users of these languages are spread globally, and countless people face and solve similar technical problems daily. When you run into a bug, you will likely find a solution within minutes of searching.
  • Mature Ecosystem & Solutions: A popular language means a rich ecosystem of third-party libraries, frameworks, and toolchains—whether it's database operations, authentication, payment integration, or deployment and maintenance, there are likely ready-made, proven solutions you can directly adopt.
  • Abundant Learning Resources: From official documentation to tech blogs, and from video courses to community Q&A, the learning paths for mature languages are extremely clear. You can spend the least amount of time filling in knowledge gaps rather than getting stuck in an information desert.

For a Web Application Full Stack, you can choose:

  • Frontend: React/Vue.js + TailwindCSS
  • Full Stack Framework: Next.js or Nuxt.js
  • Backend Service: Supabase
  • Deployment: Vercel
  • Payments: Stripe

Step 1: Success Starts Small — Launch Your Product Quickly

The biggest enemies of an indie developer are “overthinking” and “chasing perfection.” Therefore, we must establish the right mindset at the very beginning.

1. Act Fast: Replace “Perfect” with a “Preview”

Many projects die in their infancy because we always want to build a fully-featured, “perfect product” in one go.

We recommend building an MVP (Minimum Viable Product) first and launching it to the market.

An MVP is not a sloppy, half-finished product; it is “the minimum set of features that can validate the core value.” Focus only on the 1 or 2 most core features so users can experience the product's unique value proposition. For instance, the MVP of a note-taking app might just be a webpage that saves text online, rather than supporting Markdown, multi-device sync, and folder management right from the start.

Launching an MVP helps us gather user feedback, iterate on features, and adjust the development direction based on real-world inputs.

Action Guide: Set a very short development cycle (e.g., 1-2 weeks) and force yourself to launch a publicly accessible preview version. This will immediately shift you from a “thinker” to a “doer.”

Case Study: The founders of Linktree initially built a simple “link aggregation page” using basic HTML/CSS. Today, it is valued at over $1 billion. Simple technology is not a barrier to commercial success.

2. Embrace Small Ideas: Finding Gold in Niche Markets

Don't let the thought that “this idea isn't grand enough” kill your project. Most successful indie development projects start by solving an extremely tiny, concrete pain point. For example, an “image compression tool,” a “resume template generator,” or a “niche information aggregator.”

Small ideas often correspond to an underserved Niche Market. There is less competition here, user needs are clear, and you can more easily gain your first batch of loyal users and early revenue.

Success Case: JSON Formatter, a simple online tool, receives over 1 million visits monthly and generates significant revenue purely from ads.

Action Guide: Create your own “idea backlog” to record fleeting, seemingly insignificant needs. They are valuable seeds for future projects.

Step 2: Practice Makes Perfect — Launch Your Project at Zero Cost

Before validating the market, every penny must be spent wisely. Fortunately, we live in an era with an abundance of free, high-quality services.

1. Make Good Use of Free Hosting Services

  • Frontend/Full Stack Hosting: Vercel and Netlify are excellent choices for hosting modern frontend frameworks (like Next.js). They offer free CI/CD, automatically deploying on every code commit, which greatly improves efficiency.
  • Static Page Hosting: Purely static websites can be hosted using GitHub Pages or Cloudflare Pages. Cloudflare's Workers also provide a free Serverless computing environment, sufficient for supporting lightweight backends.
  • Backend/Database: BaaS (Backend-as-a-Service) platforms like Supabase or Firebase offer free tiers for databases, user authentication, and file storage, allowing developers without backend experience to quickly build fully-functional applications.

2. Let AI Be Your Efficiency Amplifier

AI tools are combat multipliers for a “solopreneur.”

  • Coding Assistants: Claude Code or Cursor can significantly speed up your coding and help you resolve complex bugs.
  • Content & Design: Use ChatGPT/Gemini to generate marketing copy and product descriptions; use Midjourney/Canva AI to generate logos, icons, and promotional graphics, easily making up for design shortcomings.

Step 3: The Growth Flywheel — Let the World Discover Your Value

Building the product is only the first step. Knowing how to let target users find and use it is a critical life-or-death factor.

Strategy 1 (Extroverted Version): Build in Public

This is not just marketing; it is a product development philosophy. You can share your journey publicly on social media (such as X/Twitter, Jike) or a personal blog:

  • Development Progress: “Today I resolved XX bug.”
  • Thinking Process: “I'm debating between features A and B, which one do you think is more important?”
  • Metrics & Stats: “First week online, gained 50 registered users!”
  • Failures & Lessons: “This marketing attempt failed, here is a post-mortem on why…”

This transparency helps you build trust, foster a community, receive instant feedback, and rapidly establish a personal brand.

After accumulating some followers, you can direct them to your private community. Discord is highly recommended for this, as it shifts communication from passive social platform feeds to active, direct sharing.

Success Case: Indie Maker Fox successfully promoted his mkdir and mksaas brands on Twitter through Build in Public, generating over $100,000 in revenue.

Strategy 2 (Introverted Version): Content is King

If you are not comfortable putting yourself out there publicly, content marketing is your best option.

  • Blog Posts / Documentation: Write high-quality blog posts centered around the problems your product solves. For example, if you built an image compression tool, you could write “10 Tips to Improve Website Loading Speed” and naturally direct readers to your product in the article.
  • SEO (Search Engine Optimization): Learn basic SEO to ensure your website and content are indexed by search engines like Google and achieve good rankings. This is the best way to acquire long-term, free, and stable traffic.

You can read Ahrefs' SEO Beginner's Guide to learn.

Building a Product Matrix

Once you have a successful Project A, you can naturally promote your new Project B on A's website, driving cross-traffic and building your own product ecosystem.

Alternatively, cross-link your different websites. Sometimes, a small, polished standalone tool page can continuously drive new users to your main project.

How to Host Micro-Tool Websites for Free?

For lightweight tool websites, you can operate at zero or very low cost:

Free Hosting Solutions:

  • Vercel/Netlify: Generous free tiers, supporting automated deployments and CDN acceleration.
  • Cloudflare Pages: Unlimited bandwidth, global CDN, and sufficient build times.
  • GitHub Pages: Perfect for static websites, seamlessly integrating with GitHub.

Cost Analysis:
Owning a professional domain name of your own is key to building a brand and trust. This is the only fixed cost, typically under $20/year. For the most common .com domain, the annual fee is around $10-12 (Cloudflare renewal price is $10.44/year).

If you choose to monetize via Google AdSense, using a conservative $1 CPM (Cost Per Mille, cost per thousand impressions):

  • You need 20,000 annual visits to break even.
  • This averages only 1,700 visits per month.
  • Considering an ad blocker rate of about 25-30%, you practically need around 2,200 monthly visits.

    A .com domain only requires 1,200 monthly visits.

Although the number is down to 1,200 visits/month, without an initial following, it is still hard to achieve in the early stages. This requires us to practice SEO optimization, continue content creation, and constantly refine our tools so they truly solve users' needs. The rest is a matter of time and patience.

Step 4: From Traffic to Revenue — Monetization Strategies Explained

Once you have users and traffic, the next step is converting them into actual revenue. Here are several proven monetization models:

Subscription (SaaS)

Applicable Scenarios: Tools or services that provide ongoing value.
Pricing Strategy:

  • Free Tier: Basic features + usage limits
  • Paid Tier: $5-50/month, unlocking all features
  • Enterprise Tier: $100+/month, supporting team collaboration

Success Case: Notion grew from $0 to a $10 billion valuation, with its core being the “free trial + paid subscription” model.

One-time Payment

Applicable Scenarios: Utility software, templates, courses.
Pricing Strategy: $10-200 one-time fee.
Advantages: Low user mental barrier, high conversion rate.

Ad Revenue

Applicable Scenarios: High-traffic free tools.
Revenue Expectation: Approximately $1-5 per 1,000 visits using Google AdSense.
Advantages: Free for users, low entry barrier.

Want to view website visitor data for free? Check out: Useful Websites: View Website Traffic Data for Free via traffic.cv

Affiliate Marketing / Referral Commission

Applicable Scenarios: Content websites, tool recommendations.
Revenue Method: Recommend relevant products to earn 5-50% commission.
Platforms: Amazon Associates, Taobao Alliance, etc.

Hybrid Model

Most successful indie developers use a combination of monetization methods:

  • Core paid features + ad revenue
  • Free tool to drive traffic + paid premium version
  • Product sales + consulting services

Summary: Stop Waiting, Start Creating Today

Let's review our roadmap: Start with a tiny idea, build an MVP quickly using free tools, then find your first users via building in public or content marketing, and finally, after validating success, consider paid growth and building a product matrix.

The path of indie development begins with a single step. Select the simplest idea from your “idea backlog,” set a one-week goal, take action immediately, and publish your first “preview version” today!

Your future story starts being written today.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1: I don't know how to code at all. Can I really do indie development?

A: Absolutely! There are many no-code/low-code platforms, and AI coding assistants are improving daily. The technical barrier has been greatly lowered, and people with non-technical backgrounds can completely build products.

Q2: How much starting capital do I need?

A: Almost zero. A domain name costs $10-15 a year, and other services (hosting, databases, payments) have free tiers that are more than enough for the MVP stage. The real investment is your time and energy.

Q3: How long before I see revenue?

A: This varies by project. Simple utility products might see their first revenue in weeks, while complex SaaS products might take 3-6 months. The key is to validate market demand quickly rather than building a perfect product.

Q4: What if nobody uses my product?

A: This is completely normal! Most products go through a “nobody knows about it” stage. The key is:

  1. Make sure you are solving a real problem.
  2. Find where your target users gather.
  3. Continuously improve the product experience.

Q5: Can one person really make a successful product?

A: Of course! Many famous products were created by solo developers: Nomad List ($500K annual revenue), the early version of Buffer, etc. The key is to focus on core features and avoid trying to do too much.

Q6: Should I build a mobile App or a website?

A: For indie developers, it is recommended to start with a website. Web applications have low development costs, fast iteration speeds, require no app store approval, and user acquisition is more direct. Consider mobile versions after the product is validated.

Q7: The competition is too fierce. Is there still a chance?

A: There is always a chance! The market is large enough; the key is to find a niche demand. Even in seemingly saturated markets, there are always underserved user groups. Focus on a specific user segment, deliver a great user experience, and you will find your place.
